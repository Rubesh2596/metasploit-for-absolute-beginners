# Auxiliary Modules

## 1. What are auxiliary modules?

Auxiliary modules are used for tasks such as scanning, enumeration, or service inspection.

### Why do we need it?

They help gather information and understand what is available on a target.

### Simple explanation

An auxiliary module is not necessarily an exploit. It often helps you inspect or assess a target.

### Example categories

- Scanners
- Enumeration modules
- Service checks

### Practice exercise

Use `search` to find a scanning or auxiliary module and read its description.

### Common mistakes

- Thinking every auxiliary module is an exploit.
- Forgetting that scanning can still affect systems and must be authorized.

### Recap

Auxiliary modules are often used for information gathering rather than direct exploitation.

## 2. How they differ from exploit modules

Exploit modules focus on taking advantage of a vulnerability. Auxiliary modules usually inspect or gather information.

### Why do we need it?

The distinction matters because not every module is meant to trigger a security flaw.

### Example

- Exploit: target a known weakness.
- Auxiliary: inspect a service or collect data.

### Practice exercise

Compare the description of an exploit and an auxiliary module.

### Recap

The difference is purpose: exploitation versus observation and diagnosis.

### Review questions

1. What is the main difference?
2. Why is this distinction important?
3. Why is authorization still important for auxiliary modules?
